MSK

The Legacy of the Manhattan Subway Kings

MSK was forged in the fall of 1983. Founded by the original quartet of CHI, FURN, MADE, and WASE—all alumni of JH52 (1980–1984)—the collective was christened by MADE as the “Manhattan Subway Kids.”

What began as a group of ambitious youths commanding the Dyckman (200th St) layups quickly evolved into a dominant force. We didn’t just participate in the culture; we fought for it, battling crews from Broadway to Brooklyn for total supremacy. As our reputation grew, so did our reach. We aligned with heavy-hitting uptown syndicates, including the Manhattan Rebels and Renegades (MR), effectively holding down the blocks from 200th to 207th Street, from Broadway to Post Avenue.

Through these strategic mergers, the Manhattan Subway Kings were born—a massive, disciplined crew that became an undeniable power in every yard north of 168th Street. From 175, 200, and 225 to the bus, sanitation, and ghost yards, MSK was the standard.

Evolution and Global Reach

While the “Great Train Scrub” of the late 1980s and early 90s signaled the end of the traditional subway era, it did not signal the end of MSK. As the culture transitioned from the tunnels to the walls, our influence remained constant. MSK has never ended; it has only expanded.
